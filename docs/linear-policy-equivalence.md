# Linear-policy equivalence under exact Gaussian conditioning

The residual-projection and explicitly A-conjugate policies use different bases for the same cumulative observation subspace.

At iteration `k`, the residual policy uses a direction `r_k`. The A-conjugate policy replaces that direction by

\[
\tilde r_k = r_k - \sum_{j<k} \alpha_j s_j,
\]

where the coefficients are chosen so that

\[
s_j^\top A \tilde r_k = 0.
\]

This changes the basis but not the span generated by the current residual together with the previously observed directions.

For exact noiseless linear observations of the form

\[
S^\top A x = S^\top b,
\]

Gaussian conditioning depends on the linear information subspace spanned by the columns of `S`, not on a particular nonsingular basis of that subspace. Replacing `S` by `S R` for an invertible matrix `R` gives the same affine constraint set and therefore the same exact posterior.

Consequently, when both iterative policies generate the same cumulative observation span and perform the same number of informative projections, their posterior means and covariances should agree up to floating-point error even though the individual search directions differ.

This equivalence is not expected to hold automatically when:

- observations are noisy or approximate;
- directions are discarded or truncated;
- numerical degeneracy thresholds cause one policy to stop earlier;
- covariance approximations are introduced;
- the policies use different information subspaces;
- stopping rules terminate at different projection counts.

The comparison tests therefore verify posterior equivalence rather than declaring a winner between two basis representations of the same exact information.
